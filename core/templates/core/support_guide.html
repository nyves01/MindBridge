BE THERE FOR SOMEONE

Sometimes listening makes all the difference.

Learn simple ways to support a friend, family member, or someone you care about who may be experiencing emotional or mental health difficulties.

UNDERSTANDING AND CONNECTION

You don't need all the answers to offer support.

Supporting someone begins with kindness, patience, and a willingness to listen. Small actions can help people feel heard, respected, and less alone.

PRACTICAL SUPPORT GUIDANCE

Ways you can make a difference.

Consider these approaches when someone you care about is going through a difficult time.

Listen Without Judging

Give the person time to express themselves. Listen patiently without interrupting or immediately trying to solve everything.

Show Empathy

Acknowledge their feelings and let them know that you care. Avoid comparing their experience with someone else's.

Choose Your Words Carefully

Simple phrases such as "I'm here for you" or "Would you like to talk?" can help someone feel supported.

Respect Their Privacy

Treat personal conversations with respect. Do not share someone's private experiences without their permission, except when necessary to protect immediate safety.

Encourage Professional Support

When appropriate, gently encourage the person to speak with a qualified mental health professional or healthcare provider.

Remember Your Own Well-being

Supporting another person can be challenging. Maintain your own boundaries and seek support when you need it.

A LITTLE THOUGHT GOES A LONG WAY

Things to avoid when supporting someone.

Avoid dismissing their feelings, blaming them, making assumptions, forcing them to talk, or promising that everything will immediately be okay. Focus on listening and understanding instead.

YOU CAN MAKE A DIFFERENCE

Being present is already a meaningful step.

You do not have to manage someone's mental health alone. If they may be in immediate danger, seek emergency assistance rather than handling the situation by yourself.

Remember

This guide provides general educational information. It does not replace professional mental health advice, assessment, or treatment.
